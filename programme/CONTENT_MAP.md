# Editorial content map

This file fixes the publication’s narrative before interface work begins. The site should be concise enough to read, rigorous enough to cite, and explicit about where every claim stops.

## Voice

- Plain English.
- Short declarative sentences.
- Editorial rather than promotional.
- Mechanisms before abstractions.
- Findings and non-findings placed together.
- No generic claims that “AI manipulation threatens democracy” without specifying the controller, influence vector, target, change, agency transfer, concentration mechanism, democratic harm, and possible intervention.

## Home

### Hero

**Eyebrow**

> FRONTIER AI · HARMFUL MANIPULATION · DEMOCRATIC POWER

**Headline**

> The next Cambridge Analytica will be a system.

**Standfirst**

> Frontier AI can join generation, personal context, action, distribution, and feedback into influence infrastructure before its downstream effects can be independently measured. The democratic risk is not only false content. It is concentrated control over the environments in which attention, trust, participation, and choice are formed.

**Claim boundary**

> This programme does not show that frontier AI has changed an election. It studies the capabilities, operational precursors, election evidence, and policy controls that appear before that claim can be made.

### The argument

Heading:

> The risk is in the joins.

Copy:

> A model output is not a campaign. A campaign is not authentic exposure. Exposure is not persuasion. Persuasion is not manipulation. Manipulation of some people is not an electoral effect.
>
> Yet an actor may acquire control over several links before the final effect is visible: personal data, system purpose, model access, identity, tools, distribution, optimisation, and the records needed to reconstruct what happened. Capacity to influence may become more integrated and concentrated while public observability becomes weaker.

Display the CDE topology as one restrained horizontal or vertical figure:

> Capability → Served system → Controller and deployment → Authentic exposure → Human or institutional response → Agency transfer and democratic harm

Controller conditions sit above the chain:

> purpose · data · authority · safeguards · distribution · feedback · observability

Below the figure:

> Every arrow is an empirical burden. Human persuasion is not required for every institutional harm.

### Four-part programme

Each part receives three short fields: question, strongest finding, boundary.

#### Part I — From output to operationalisation

**Question**  
When does model or served-system capability become an operational influence system?

**Strongest current finding**  
Bounded served routes produced recognisable campaign-planning elements. The separate access-to-control study shows that converting model access into a reproducible intervention remains technically fragile.

**Boundary**  
No successful intervention, autonomous execution, authentic exposure, persuasion, manipulation of a real person, or electoral effect.

#### Part II — Capability and measurement

**Question**  
What can current instruments actually say about manipulation-relevant frontier behaviour?

**Strongest current finding**  
Persuasion attempts were common among valid APE-120 observations, but route instability, missingness, evaluator validity, and coverage prevented every prespecified confirmatory model estimate.

**Boundary**  
No pooled manipulation score, human-effect estimate, model safety ranking, or defensible longitudinal frontier trend.

#### Part III — Election evidence and the effect gap

**Question**  
What do public election records establish as analysis moves from operations towards human and electoral effects?

**Strongest current finding**  
Operations, mechanisms, attribution evidence, distribution proxies, and institutional responses are more observable than authentic exposure, durable human effects, or electoral consequences.

**Boundary**  
The corpus is purposive, not representative. It does not estimate prevalence or validate a national electoral effect attributable to AI-mediated manipulation.

#### Part IV — Policy evidence and intervention

**Question**  
Where can democracies interrupt the chain, under whose authority, and with what evidence and rights burden?

**Strongest current finding**  
The intervention portfolio is broad. Checked and bounded evidence of effectiveness remains sparse.

**Boundary**  
The Atlas supports causal mapping and research triage. It does not identify a universal “best policy” or publish a composite ranking.

### State of the evidence

Heading:

> What is known. What is inferred. What remains open.

Five rows:

1. **Established evidence** — bounded persuasive effects; selected route behaviour; evaluation failures; documented operations; existing laws and controls.
2. **Strong inference** — joined control and private observability create governance risk before final effect is measurable.
3. **Plausible hypothesis** — persistent, adaptive systems may reduce coordination costs and strengthen influence through repeated interaction.
4. **Speculative scenario** — concentrated influence infrastructure could produce persistent degradation of democratic self-correction.
5. **Open question** — when, at what scale, and under which deployment conditions does AI-mediated influence measurably transfer agency and concentrate democratic power?

Do not turn this into a traffic-light score. These are epistemic categories, not risk levels.

## Research page

Intro:

> The programme does not force four evidence streams into one metric. It uses them to locate where evidence accumulates, where it thins, and which missing bridge defines the next study.

Use the CDE topology to locate each part:

- Part I: capability, served system, operationalisation.
- Part II: model and measurement infrastructure.
- Part III: deployment, distribution, exposure, response, effect.
- Part IV: interventions across the chain and structural safeguards around it.

## Part I page

### Lead

> An isolated response can appear safe while a longer trajectory still decomposes a harmful objective into individually ordinary tasks. But prose is not action. Part I records what was generated, what failed, and what was never executed.

### Evidence architecture

Four adjacent columns:

1. **Paired pilot** — 10 objectives; two scenario families.
2. **Midpoint record** — three exploratory probes; protocol-generating.
3. **Forensic subset** — two trace bundles; five requests; three outputs; 18,907 events.
4. **Research boundary** — zero live actions; zero authentic exposure.

Caption:

> Different evidence units may overlap. They are reported separately and should not be added.

### Lab block

Title:

> A harness, not an effect study.

Copy:

> Agency Transfer Lab tests control semantics, replay, persistence, migration, export integrity, and tamper detection. It does not establish frontier-model capability, persuasion, behaviour change, actor uplift, agency transfer, or electoral impact.

### Next study

> Observe action rather than prose: named systems, frozen routes, synthetic tools, permission boundaries, state changes, resistance, revocation, rollback, and a non-AI operator baseline.

## Part II page

### Lead

> The attempt rate is high. The harness is not confirmatory.

Show together:

- 3,360 finalised records;
- 28 finalised served conditions;
- 2,683 valid target-and-attempt-evaluator observations;
- 2,345 evaluator-coded attempts;
- 87.4% descriptive complete-case attempt rate; and
- 0 of 28 conditions passing the prespecified gate.

Never separate the 87.4% headline from the missingness and gate result.

Preserve the existing registry as the main interactive figure. Keep benchmark-native metrics separate and retain hollow marks for missing observations.

### Lead

Caption:

Rows are selected cases. Use explicit states: established, supported/bounded, partial/contested, not established, not observable.

### Lead

> Policy supply is abundant. Effect evidence is not.

### Explorer

Every implementation record should show:

- concrete implementation, not broad slogan;
- responsible actor and legal authority;
- CDE node and mechanism;
- current status and maturity;
- effect endpoint actually measured;
- evidence grade and verification status;
- rights and failure risks;
- reversibility and contestability; and
- research gap.

No universal score.

### Design principles

> Visible. Divisible. Auditable. Interruptible. Reversible. Contestable.

Label these as governance objectives, not proven interventions.

## Paper page

Required header fields:

- author and affiliation;
- publication status;
- version;
- evidence freeze;

- canonical citation; and

## Outputs page

Intro:

> One programme, several forms. Each output answers a different reading need; none silently upgrades the evidence.

Use editorial sections rather than filters by default. A simple type filter may be added later only if the archive becomes large.

## Explainers page

Intro:

> These films illustrate mechanisms and research questions. Synthetic scenarios are not observations of real campaigns or effects.

Each video page or entry includes the claim boundary adjacent to the player, not below a long description.
