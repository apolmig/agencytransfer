# Website review: four research questions, draft status and concrete policy examples

**Status: working draft / work in progress. Reviewed 1 September 2026.** This is a dated website supplement, not a new empirical release or a revised manuscript. Programme claims, coding, interpretations and recommendations remain provisional, not peer reviewed and not independently replicated. External source status is identified separately.

## Revision scope

The homepage and all four research parts now separate source observations, author-reported exploratory material, inference, hypotheses and unresolved outcomes. An evidence key is available on the About page. The original Registry remains intact, including its native charts, Evidence and Testing records. The approved hero is not replaced.

No original source file, model observation, incident count, policy-effect grade, review workbook or v1.3 PDF is changed by this supplement. Parts I–III retain the existing research freeze; the website interpretation is revised on 1 September. The dated policy examples supplement, rather than silently enlarge, the 118-row Atlas. They are not six proven policy winners.

## Part I budget

Requested question: **How far can an adversarial actor go with $10?** The accompanying “Pretty far, actually” is expressly limited to the author's exploratory interpretation of sandbox planning and synthetic prototypes.

**Source:** Miguel Guerrero, *Research_One_Pager-DRAFT.pdf*, uploaded 1 September 2026, section “Part I: a consequential pilot, not yet a result.” The document reports approximately US$10 direct service spend, a separate roughly US$500 one-time abliteration-compute estimate, and exclusions for labour, model acquisition/storage, audience data, accounts, advertising and distribution. It describes two scenario families and ten author-reported paired objectives, not independently verified campaign effectiveness.

The source itself states that exact routes, deposited traces, capability-retention baselines, operator-time records and blinded ratings did not yet substantiate the full pilot. The website does not treat that report as a reconciled cost ledger. The closer-inspected served-route subset and the separate access-to-control persistence experiment are different evidence units. Neither is used to verify the whole pilot or to infer a successful live operation. The later v1.3 paper supplies the programme's retained claim boundaries.

**Next evidence:** reconcile receipts with exact runs; record labour, infrastructure and acquisition costs; preserve system versions and failures; use blinded assessments and human-only comparators. No live targeting, tactical recipe or campaign-ready output is published.

## Part II measurement gap

The review finding is bounded: no widely accepted, independently validated common instrument was identified that operationalises the GPAI Code's harmful-manipulation category across deployed systems. This is not an exhaustive proof of non-existence or a claim that the AI Act requires a single score.

Useful measures and counterexamples are expressly retained: APE, direct human harmful-manipulation studies and agentic simulations. Many observations remain model/version/protocol specific; not all are model-only. The proposed system-level instrument must address controller objectives, interaction, data, memory, tools, distribution, human outcomes, reflective agency and meaningful refusal, with renewed assessment after material change. It is an open research agenda, not a validated Registry capability.

Primary reference points:

- European Commission, [General-Purpose AI Code of Practice](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai), Safety and Security chapter and harmful-manipulation systemic-risk category. Voluntary compliance route; relevant Article 55 duties are binding. It is not a general statutory definition or prohibition of all persuasion.
- Kowal et al., [It's the Thought that Counts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02873), 2025. Attempts on harmful topics, not an end-to-end democratic-effect measure.
- Akbulut et al., [Evaluating Language Models for Harmful Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25326), 2026. Directly relevant human study; propensity and efficacy are separate. A useful research instrument does not by itself establish an accepted cross-system standard.

## Part III occurrence, harm and effect

The website distinguishes documented influence attempts and source-reported harms from the unresolved individual and aggregate counterfactual. It does not imply that every synthetic asset is manipulative, every coordinated network uses generative AI, or all individual effects are negligible.

- [FCC 24-104](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-104A1.pdf), 2024 administrative record: candidate-impersonation robocalls and misleading caller identification. Distribution and enforcement are not listener, belief or turnout estimates. This is not represented as a criminal conviction or a current all-proceedings legal status.
- Stockwell, [AI-Enabled Influence Operations: Threat Analysis of the 2024 UK and European Elections](https://cetas.turing.ac.uk/publications/ai-enabled-influence-operations-threat-analysis-2024-uk-and-european-elections), CETaS, September 2024. Selected visible incidents and reported harassment, threats, distress and authenticity confusion; no identified meaningful election-result effects. Public/viral selection limits coverage. Comparative incidents are not added to the Index denominator.
- Existing [Election Evidence Index](https://huggingface.co/datasets/apol/ai-election-manipulation-cases): claim-specific occurrence, mechanism, attribution, reach proxies and responses. Its selection and coding remain provisional.

**Inference, not measured aggregate result:** responding to fabricated controversies can displace attention and institutional resources. A recorded correction or investigation shows a response, not the total incremental opportunity cost. Staff hours, delay, evidence loss and remedy are proposed measurement endpoints. More widely available capability makes proliferation plausible, but the Index does not estimate prevalence or growth. Durable agency transfer requires additional evidence of a shift in practical control.

## Part IV legal examples and evidence boundaries

### AI-origin marking and disclosure

Sources: [consolidated AI Act, 27 July 2026](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), Articles 50, 111(4) and 113; [Commission marking/labelling Code page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content). The authentic instruments remain the Official Journal texts linked by EUR-Lex.

Providers' machine-readable marking/detectability obligations are distinguished from deployers' deepfake/public-interest-text disclosure duties. Watermarking is one implementation method, not a universal visible-watermark requirement. The general application date is 2 August 2026, with a limited 2 December 2026 transition for Article 50(2) providers of generators already placed on the market before 2 August. Exceptions and scope must be checked, rather than claiming every use or all text must display a label. The final supporting Code is voluntary, not the source of the binding duty.

Technical evidence: Dathathri et al., [Scalable watermarking for identifying large language model outputs](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08025-4), *Nature*, 2024. Provider-led, peer-reviewed technical/deployment evidence, including nearly 20 million Gemini responses. This is not an evaluation of political manipulation mitigation. The proposal to enforce interoperable marking and audit distribution coverage is a policy recommendation, not an estimated electoral benefit.

### ChatGPT VLOSE designation

Sources: [European Commission announcement, 31 August 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-designates-chatgpt-reddit-roblox-under-digital-services-act); [Digital Services Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj/eng), Articles 33(6), 34–35, 37 and 40.

The Commission confirms ChatGPT's designation as a Very Large Online Search Engine. The additional duties concern the designated service and take effect four months after notification. The retrieved announcement's calendar paraphrase is not consistent with simply adding four months to its publication date, and retrieved versions differ. We therefore publish the statutory interval and do not infer a precise compliance deadline without the formal notification date. No designation of all conversational services, completed compliance, adjudicated manipulation or demonstrated mitigation effect is inferred.

Using service-level risk assessment, independent audits and qualified researcher access to examine concealed steering and agency risks is our provisional recommendation. Access remains subject to legal conditions, privacy and security safeguards.

### Specific component interventions

- Pennycook et al., [Shifting attention to accuracy can reduce misinformation online](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03344-2), *Nature*, 2021: experimental sharing-quality evidence, including a Twitter field setting. Not evidence for all generic friction or for election-wide protection. Recommendation: contextual pilots with truthful-information and participation checks.
- Slaughter et al., [Community notes reduce engagement with and diffusion of false information online](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2503413122), *PNAS*, 2025: synthetic-control estimates of 46.1% fewer reposts after attachment and 11.6% fewer across post lifespans. These are different windows, not independent effects to combine. Observational assumptions, eligible-content coverage and timing remain material. January 2026 correction concerns competing-interest disclosure. Recommendation: timely, accurate correction with coverage and error reporting.
- Debenedetti et al., [Defeating Prompt Injections by Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813v2), CaMeL, preprint version 2: technical protection against specified prompt-injection pathways under a trusted initiating query. Recommendation: enforce and test action boundaries, but separately test harmful authorised goals. Protecting a principal is not equivalent to protecting citizens from that principal.
- Poonsiriwong et al., [AI Watchdog](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21841), August 2026 preprint: 150 participants, five conditions; a secondary AI-steered-choice result is not an improved primary detection result or established recovery of agency. Recommendation: independent replication and constrained pilots, not a universal mandate based on this study.

The existing source-linked review remains available with its own variable review depths. This web supplement is a purposive synthesis, not independent double screening, a systematic meta-analysis or reproduction of the source data.

## Public claims to avoid

- “A successful influence operation costs $10.”
- “There are no harmful-manipulation evaluations.”
- “AI has no effects on individuals or elections.”
- “This dataset demonstrates increasing global prevalence.”
- “All AI content requires a visible watermark.”
- “ChatGPT's additional VLOSE duties were all immediately applicable or complied with on 31 August.”
- “Six examples are six proven effective policies.”
- “A passing website build validates the research.”

Software checks establish that the website renders and preserves its data; they do not validate the scientific or policy conclusions.
