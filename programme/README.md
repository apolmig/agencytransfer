# Agency Transfer Research Programme

This directory is the editorial and publication control record for Miguel Guerrero’s ERA:AI research programme on frontier AI, harmful manipulation, democracy, and concentrated power.

The public site is not a product dashboard. It is a maintained research record: a concise account of the programme’s argument, evidence, limits, publications, datasets, methods, visual outputs, and explainers.

## Editorial spine

**Agency Transfer Research Programme**  
*Frontier AI, harmful manipulation, and democratic power*

**Flagship paper**  
*Harmful Manipulation and Election Security: The Capability–Deployment–Effect Gap*

> The next Cambridge Analytica will be a system.

The programme studies manipulation at scale facilitated by frontier AI: what is happening, what may be coming, and how democracies can mitigate the danger. Its wider lens is manipulation as a mechanism for the extreme concentration of political and epistemic power.

The central discipline is simple: capability is not deployment; deployment is not authentic exposure; exposure is not effect. Evidence at one layer does not silently license a claim at the next. The reverse error is also wrong: an evidenced upstream mechanism does not become harmless merely because final vote effects remain unknown.

## Four research parts

| Part | Research object | Strongest current contribution | Claim ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. From output to operationalisation | Served systems, exploratory probes, trace bundles, and access-to-control methods | Bounded routes can produce campaign-planning elements; technical conversion to reproducible intervention remains fragile | No successful intervention, autonomous execution, authentic exposure, persuasion, or electoral effect |
| II. Capability and measurement | Evaluation Registry and recovered APE-120 audit | Persuasion attempts are common among valid observations, but the measurement infrastructure remains non-confirmatory | No pooled manipulation score, human-effect estimate, model ranking, or defensible frontier trend |
| III. Election evidence | Claim-level public evidence index | Operations, mechanisms, attribution, distribution proxies, and institutional responses are more observable than human or electoral effects | No prevalence estimate or validated national electoral effect |
| IV. Policy evidence | Causal policy atlas | Policy supply is broad; checked and bounded effect evidence is sparse | No global policy-effect estimate or composite “best policy” ranking |

## Status model

- **published** — a stable public asset exists at its canonical URL.
- **release candidate** — the content exists, but canonical selection or durable publication is incomplete.
- **working** — active research material that may be public but is not a frozen release.
- **hosting required** — a media master exists only on temporary or non-canonical hosting.
- **withheld/private** — retained for controlled validation or responsible-release reasons and never linked publicly.
- **legacy/superseded** — preserved for provenance but excluded from the current public programme.

## Update rule

Every material change should update the manifest first. The site should then render or import that record rather than reproduce counts, versions, and links independently.

A publication update is incomplete unless it records:

1. the exact source and public version;
2. the evidence cutoff;
3. the denominator behind every count;
4. the strongest supported claim;
5. the claim ceiling;
6. the canonical public URL; and
7. any responsible-release restriction.

## Responsible release

The public programme may publish concepts, aggregates, source-linked claims, non-operational figures, defensive methods, and synthetic explainers. It must not publish campaign-ready outputs, targetable profiles, evasion instructions, live credentials, raw harmful traces, or private validation material.

Synthetic explainers must be labelled as illustrations. They are not evidence of a real operation, authentic exposure, behaviour change, or electoral effect.
