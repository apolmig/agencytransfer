# What works at the point of influence—and who the safeguard protects
## Part IV evidence review · 1 September 2026

The policy conclusion should no longer be “we cannot identify what works.” There is usable evidence for specific interface changes, accuracy-oriented sharing interventions, some corrective context, and enforceable technical boundaries. There is also evidence that superficially similar interventions fail on different endpoints. The useful comparison is therefore not six winners—or five winners—but **which control changes which decision, under whose authority, and with what evidence of benefit to the affected person**.

This review annotates all 118 existing implementations against 69 selected source records. Eight sources received new main-text methods/results or substantive-section checks; the others have explicitly recorded depths, including primary abstracts, publication summaries, official text, and six retained prior adjudications. The research is a single-pass source-linked assessment, not independent double screening or full-text extraction of every cited study. The historical dataset's six checked effect claims are not silently upgraded. Three additional implementation candidates are documented separately and do not change the 118-row denominator.

### 1. Awareness, resistance and agency are different outcomes

Warning experiments now make the distinction concrete. A 2026 sycophancy preprint found changes in perceptions of the assistant without a reliable corresponding improvement in its influence-related outcomes. The null result is not proof of zero effect, and its interpersonal setting is not an election. [R040]

In the AI Watchdog preprint, a small five-arm experiment reported a reduction in AI-steered choices with a dismissible just-in-time intervention. That was a preregistered secondary endpoint; the primary detection outcome did not improve. This is a replication priority, not deployment-ready proof. [R039]

The analytical implication is stronger than “labels sometimes fail.” Recognition, calibrated trust, informed disagreement and user-directed action need separate measures. An intervention can change behaviour without improving awareness. That does not automatically make it beneficial: the monitor could simply replace the first system as the actor shaping choice. Preserving agency requires attention to the person's own goals, reasons, practical refusal and ability to contest the intervention.

Commercial persuasion studies provide a related warning: conspicuous sponsorship can increase detection without clearly neutralising steering. Their disclosure comparison was conducted in a separate wave, not one simultaneous five-arm experiment. Do not present its numerical contrast as an unqualified causal estimate of disclosure efficacy. [R041]

**Revision:** retain disclosure for notice and accountability, but do not count disclosure, recognition or reduced trust as demonstrated protection of agency. Separate the operator's objective from the user's objective; evaluate defensive monitors as influence systems too.

### 2. Friction is not one treatment

A Twitter interface change studied by Ershov and Morales reduced news sharing, but did not selectively reduce low-factualness content relative to more factual material. Its effects differed across the studied audiences. That is a warning against treating less diffusion as an unqualified democratic benefit, not evidence that WhatsApp forwarding limits necessarily fail. [R003]

Accuracy prompts target a different mechanism: attention to factual accuracy rather than undifferentiated effort. Controlled and field evidence supports bounded improvements in sharing quality; meta-analyses and adversarial reanalyses must not be counted as independent replications when they reuse observations. [R006–R008]

The existing WhatsApp evidence includes a diffusion simulation and an observational audit of duplicated-media labels. Neither is a field counterfactual for the exact forwarding cap. [R004–R005]

**Revision:** remove forwarding friction from a homogeneous “best-supported” set. Add accuracy-focused prompts as a distinct candidate. Evaluate truthful information, legitimate participation, delay, duplicates and displacement—not only the quantity of content shared.

### 3. Effectiveness conditional on delivery is not effectiveness of the programme

Community Notes research illustrates the importance of the denominator. One synthetic-control study estimates substantial reductions after a note is attached, but smaller reductions when including a post's earlier lifetime engagement. Other work asks about rollout-wide outcomes. These are different estimands, not results that should be averaged into a single verdict. A 2026 correction to the first paper adds a competing-interest disclosure. [R009–R010]

A timely factual correction is also not the same treatment as an “AI-generated” label. Origin labelling research does not establish a general reduction in political persuasive influence. [R017–R018]

**Revision:** separately measure whether a control reaches the relevant content or person, when it arrives, whether it is correct, and what happens afterwards. A strong effect among treated posts can coexist with weak population protection because treatment arrives late or misses most relevant cases. Do not infer an electoral outcome from engagement.

### 4. Protecting the agent is not necessarily protecting the citizen

CaMeL provides technical evidence for enforced control and data-flow boundaries under a defined prompt-injection threat model. It treats the initiating query as trusted and explicitly leaves some text-only influence outside scope. [R037]

This creates a decisive distinction for the ERA project. A system can be resistant to outsiders hijacking the operator's instructions while faithfully executing an operator's harmful objective. Security testing can therefore protect the party whose influence power needs scrutiny.

The implication is not to reject least privilege. It is to specify **whose authority is bounded**. A rule that the operator can silently rewrite cannot by itself protect citizens against that operator. Testing external attack resistance, unauthorised tool use, concealed authorised steering and institutional dependence requires different scenarios.

**Revision:** move enforceable permissions and revocation ahead of vague model promises for the unauthorised-action threat. Do not present technical security success as a demonstration of democratic safety. Add independent constraints on purpose, incentives, data use and consequential authority.

### 5. Exit has to work after the relationship has accumulated

Hands-on portability research shows why receipt of an archive is a weak endpoint: obtaining data did not establish a functional transfer to another provider. [R033–R034] Search-market field evidence also distinguishes active choice, experience of an alternative and defaults; its modelled policy counterfactuals are not observed treatment effects. [R035]

The inference for persistent assistants is that exit should be assessed as a capability, not a button. Can a person or institution carry on the relevant task after changing providers? What happens to accumulated context, permissions, derived profiles and institutional expertise? Exporting more personal data can itself create privacy risks.

Data minimisation remains a reasonable control over one input to influence. But an offline recommendation-quality experiment does not establish that restricting assistant memory prevents emotional dependence or restores autonomy. [R030] A longitudinal chatbot study's associations between voluntary use and worse outcomes should not be recast as randomised evidence that a memory cap works. [R042]

**Revision:** retain minimisation, usable switching and public capability as structural priorities with specific tests. Their importance to agency transfer should not be confused with a demonstrated effect size.

### 6. Technique education needs an accuracy and transfer test

The retained prebunking result concerns recognition of taught techniques. More recent checks include heterogeneous findings across techniques and a small WhatsApp-like experiment without a demonstrated sharing or cross-topic benefit. [R011–R013] A five-study programme found recognition improvements without truth-discernment gains from its emotional-manipulation inoculation alone; the combined accuracy intervention performed differently. [R014]

**Revision:** evaluate whether a training intervention improves discrimination, not merely scepticism. Include accurate content using emotional language, unfamiliar techniques, real sharing and follow-up. A citizen who distrusts everything has not necessarily recovered agency.

## A revised portfolio

| Priority | Recommended use | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Bounded human-facing controls** | Remove coercive choice architecture; distinguish accuracy prompts, factual corrections and technique education as separate treatments. | Human-component evidence exists, but effects, settings and endpoint quality differ. A regulatory ban or nationwide deployment has not thereby been evaluated. |
| **Enforced authority boundaries** | Scope tools, recipients, actions and revocation outside the model; test meaningful human approval. | Technical protection is conditional on the threat model and trusted principal. User-beneficial political or institutional effects remain a separate question. |
| **Frontier-specific defensive pilots** | Independently replicate conversational monitors and evaluate conflicts, memory, dependence and user-defined goals over time. | The most directly relevant new human results are limited or preprint evidence; do not convert them into a general mandate. |
| **Accountability and structural safeguards** | Enable independent inspection, usable exit, retained public capability, plural access and applicable remedies. | Adjacent evidence and institutional reasoning justify evaluation and proportionate action, not a universal efficacy ranking. |

Provenance, detection and notices remain useful for their specified vectors; they are not substitutes for these layers. Mandatory duties remain mandatory where applicable. A research priority is neither permission to ignore the law nor evidence that a proposed law is already in force.

## What changes in the flagship findings

**External empirical findings:** selected controls change sharing, recognition, perceptions, choices or technical violations; effects can diverge across those endpoints and settings.

**New analytical contribution:** the policy CDE Gap is a problem of mismatched outcomes, incomplete delivery and misallocated authority—not simply a shortage of interventions.

**Strong inference:** concentration increases when one actor controls both the influential assistant and the evidence or monitor used to validate its safety. Effective contestability requires a separation that is operational, not merely declared.

**Plausible hypothesis:** independently governed, user-controlled defensive interfaces combined with enforceable authority limits and viable exit may protect agency better than disclosure alone. Their combined effect remains untested.

**Open question:** whether these controls preserve reflective agency and institutional self-government under persistent, adaptive frontier-AI influence at scale.

### Suggested replacement passage

> The policy gap is not that democracies have no tools. It is that tools are often credited with outcomes they were not designed or evaluated to deliver. A label may identify origin without changing a decision. A prompt may reduce sharing without improving accuracy. A permission boundary may protect an operator's instructions without protecting the people those instructions target.
>
> The evidence therefore supports a differentiated response. Use bounded interventions where the relevant human or technical endpoint has been tested. Make consequential authority inspectable and revocable. Treat disclosure as notice, not proof of informed choice. Test functional exit, not merely export. And subject defensive monitors to the same scrutiny as the systems they monitor.
>
> The democratic objective is not maximum resistance to AI advice. It is the continued ability of citizens and institutions to understand, direct, refuse and contest the systems through which choices are made. That is the boundary between assistance and agency transfer.

## The next empirical study

Test a user-facing assistant and its defensive layer together. Keep user goals, task content and incentives explicit. Compare transparent assistance, concealed conflicting objectives and externally injected instructions as different threat models. Randomise specific controls rather than vaguely defined “guardrails.”

Primary outcomes should assess goal-consistent decisions, understanding of the relevant conflict, meaningful refusal and the ability to revise or exit. Detection, trust and satisfaction are secondary indicators, not substitutes. Include accurate helpful advice so that indiscriminate rejection counts as a possible cost. Measure errors, privacy burden and unequal cognitive load.

A longer follow-up should ask whether benefits persist and whether dependence simply shifts to the defensive provider. Use consenting participants and low-risk tasks; do not expose real voters to live manipulative political operations.

## Coverage and limitations

The attached 118-row assessment gives each implementation a source match, a revised recommendation, an individual limitation, a hypothetical example and the evidence needed next. H, T, O, A and N indicate the closest kind of evidence, not its strength or whether a policy works. “No exact effect estimate identified in the selected sources” is not a claim that no such study exists anywhere.

Source selection is purposive and English-dominant, with official EU, Brazilian and Chinese material used for specific legal or institutional context. Some studies appear in several rows because their components are relevant to several controls; those mappings are not independent experiments. Overlapping meta-analyses, source versions, self-reports, preprints and conflict disclosures are recorded.

Current legal scope has not been fully re-audited. In particular, the original high-risk AI Act timetable requires refresh against current primary law; official 2026 implementation summaries identify changes. Do not silently treat old dates as current. [R055]

There is no claim of an end-to-end electoral protection effect, comprehensive current-law verification, independent double coding or reproduction of all source data. The source ledger records exactly what was consulted and at what depth.
